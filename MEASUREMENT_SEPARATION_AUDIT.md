# Measurement-Separation Novelty Audit

## Trigger
Candidate idea: "we have not yet made the measurement that can separate the explanations."

## Result 1 — model discrimination collision
Choosing measurements/experiments that best separate rival explanations is established optimal experimental design/model discrimination. Criteria based on output distance, information gain, KL divergence, expected model elimination, and optimal control already exist.

Therefore:
- Separation Reach (SR) is not retained as a novel primitive.
- A generic "measurement that separates explanations" is not an Operativity novelty claim.

## Result 2 — Empirical Reach collision
Generative Separation Reach (GSR) and Invalidation Exposure Cost (IEC), if defined as minimum cost to expand measurement capability until a separating witness becomes available, substantially duplicate the existing Empirical Reach framework: closure-relative empirical equivalence, closure obstruction, reliable evidence cost, minimum-cost observability expansion, exact witness search, and continuous admissible synthesis.

Therefore:
- GSR is rejected as a renamed Empirical Reach quantity.
- generic IEC is rejected as a renamed/restricted Empirical Reach evidence cost.
- Operativity must not absorb Empirical Reach merely to strengthen venue positioning.

## Result 3 — dual-control / active-perception collision
The idea that an action changes both the physical/system state and the informativeness of future observations is established in dual control, active learning/control, active perception, and sensor management.

Therefore:
- "actions change future information" is not novel;
- "self-expanding observability" alone is not retained as a new primitive.

## Surviving boundary
The narrower Operativity-specific question is:

> A transformation is certified under current observations. The same transformation changes the future verifier/observation closure. Should the transformation's preservation claim be judged against that transformation-conditioned closure?

This is a certification semantics question, not a new experimental-design cost metric.

It remains represented by PC/APS/SIP/TCOP and T17. No new primitive is added.

## Q1 consequence
The proposed measurement-separation addition does not by itself raise Q1 readiness. Its generic quantitative versions collide with established model-discrimination theory and the author's existing Empirical Reach framework.

A strong Q1 upgrade would require one of:
1. independent real systems where transformation-conditioned future observability materially changes certification;
2. a controlled study showing TCOP reduces specification errors/effort against expert conventional encodings;
3. a genuinely new theorem not reducible to Empirical Reach, dual control, or standard verification.

Theory remains frozen.
